## The Problem Every POD Seller Hits First

You spend hours designing the perfect t-shirt graphic, upload it to Printful, order a sample — and when it arrives, the print is blurry, soft, or visibly pixelated. Or worse, the platform rejects the file with a "low resolution" warning before you can even check out.

This happens because most designers don't know — and no one clearly tells them — how many pixels a physical print size actually requires.

This guide changes that. Every common POD product, every platform's print area, and every required pixel dimension — in one place.

---

## Part 1: The Math Behind POD Resolution

Every print-on-demand product has a **print area** — the maximum physical size your design can cover, measured in inches. Your artwork must have enough pixels to fill that area at the required DPI.

<CalloutBox type="formula" title="The POD Resolution Formula">
  **Required Pixels = Print Area (inches) × DPI**

  Example: A 12 × 16 inch t-shirt print area at 300 DPI requires:

  Width: 12 × 300 = **3,600 pixels**

  Height: 16 × 300 = **4,800 pixels**

  Your artwork file must be at least 3,600 × 4,800 pixels at 300 DPI.
</CalloutBox>

### Minimum vs. recommended DPI

Most POD platforms list a **minimum** DPI (often 150) and a **recommended** DPI (usually 300). What does this mean in practice?

At 150 DPI, prints are acceptable when viewed from normal distances (arm's length for t-shirts, 2–3 feet for posters). Fine details, thin lines, and small text will look slightly soft.

At 300 DPI, prints are sharp at close inspection. Fine details are crisp. Text is legible at small sizes. This is the industry standard for quality POD output and what serious sellers should always target.

<CalloutBox type="tip" title="Centre Your T-Shirt Design">
  POD platforms centre your design within the print area by default. Don't fill the entire print area edge-to-edge — leave breathing room. A design that's 10×12 inches centred in a 12×16 print area looks more balanced than one that touches all edges.

For AOP products, 150 DPI is generally acceptable (the fabric texture limits perceived sharpness, and these products are often viewed from a distance). However, 300 DPI gives you maximum flexibility.

  Die-cut and kiss-cut stickers require bleed — typically 0.125" (3mm) per side — just like any other print product. ## Part 6: Platform-Specific Notes

### Printful

- Recommended: **300 DPI at print size**
- Accepted formats: PNG, JPEG, SVG (for simple designs)
- Colour space: RGB
- DTG print area: 12 × 16" for standard t-shirts (3600 × 4800 px at 300 DPI)
- Templates: Available for every product in their design tool and as downloadable PSD files
- Tip: Printful's Mockup Generator shows a realistic preview — use it to proof colours and placement before ordering

### Printify

- Recommended: **300 DPI**, minimum 150 DPI
- Accepted formats: PNG (recommended), JPEG
- Colour space: RGB
- Note: Printify is a marketplace of different print providers, each with slightly different specs. Always check the specific provider's requirements for each product.
- Tip: Some Printify providers offer 14×18" print areas — larger than Printful's standard

### Redbubble

- Minimum: **2048px on the shortest side**
- Recommended: specific dimensions per product category (download their guide)
- Accepted formats: PNG (transparent), JPEG
- Colour space: RGB
- Unique feature: One design file is used across all products — Redbubble scales and positions it per product type
- Tip: For best results across all products, upload at 7632 × 6480 px (their maximum recommended canvas size)

### Merch by Amazon

- Recommended: **300 DPI at print size**
- Standard t-shirt print area: 4500 × 5400 px (15 × 18" at 300 DPI)
- Accepted format: PNG with transparent background only
- Colour space: RGB
- Tip: Amazon's print area is larger than most competitors — design at full 4500 × 5400 px to avoid stretching

### Zazzle

- Minimum: **150 DPI**, recommended 300 DPI
- Accepted formats: PNG, JPEG, PDF, AI, EPS
- Colour space: RGB or CMYK
- Unique feature: Zazzle supports vector PDF uploads (AI, EPS converted to PDF) — allowing infinitely scalable designs

## Part 7: Workflow for Creating POD-Ready Artwork

### Step 1: Research the product specifications first

Before opening your design software, look up the exact print area and DPI requirements for your specific product on your chosen platform. Download their template file if available.

### Step 2: Create your canvas at the correct dimensions

In Photoshop: **File → New** → enter width and height in pixels at the required DPI (e.g., 3600 × 4800 px at 300 PPI for a standard t-shirt front).

In Illustrator: Create an artboard at the correct inch dimensions, then ensure you export at 300 DPI (in Export for Screens or Export As → PNG → 300 DPI).

### Step 3: Use vector elements for text and logos

Vector shapes and text in your design will export crisp at any resolution. If you place a raster photo as part of the design, ensure that photo is at sufficient resolution for its placement size.

### Step 4: Export as PNG with transparency

For apparel: transparent PNG so your design sits cleanly on any shirt colour.
For posters: JPEG at maximum quality (no transparency needed for a full-bleed background).

### Step 5: Use the platform mockup generator

Always preview your design on the platform's product mockup before ordering a sample. Check:
- Is the design the right size (not too small, not bleeding off the edges)?
- Are the colours accurate?
- Is text legible at the rendered size?

### Step 6: Order one sample before bulk production

Always order a physical sample before making your product publicly available. Screen colours and printed colours can differ significantly — especially blacks (which can print as dark grey) and vibrant colours (which may be muted by DTG inks on dark fabrics).

<CalloutBox type="tip" title="Design for the Worst Case">
  When designing for multiple POD platforms simultaneously, design at the largest required canvas and for the most demanding product first. You can always reduce a large image. You can never scale up a small one without losing quality.
</CalloutBox>

## Key Takeaways

- POD requires **300 DPI at the full print area size** for sharp professional results. 150 DPI is the minimum for acceptable quality.
- A standard DTG t-shirt print area (12 × 16") requires at least **3600 × 4800 pixels** at 300 DPI.
- Upload **PNG with transparent background** for apparel; JPEG is fine for posters.
- Always use RGB colour mode for POD platforms.
- Download the platform's template for each product — it saves hours of repositioning and guess-work.
- Order a physical sample before going live. What looks perfect on screen can look different in print.
